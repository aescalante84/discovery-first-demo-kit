# Customer brief: Northfield Snack Co. (fictional)

**Who they are:** Regional snack manufacturer. Frozen and shelf-stable products sold through grocery, convenience, and foodservice distributors across several states.

**Scale (synthetic):** ~$1.1B annual revenue, ~6,000 retail doors served through distributors, 9 plants and co-packers.

**Why they'd take the meeting:** Leadership has publicly committed to cost savings and margin recovery. A sponsor on the commercial side wants to see whether AI can improve planning and promotion decisions.

## Stakeholders

| Role | Likely stance | What they own |
|---|---|---|
| CEO | Sponsor of the margin story | Cost and growth commitments to the board |
| CRO | Skeptical of anything that adds work for reps | Distributor and retailer relationships, promo spend |
| VP RevOps | Day-to-day champion | Planning process, data quality, tooling |
| CTO | Gatekeeper | Integrations, security, data access |
| CFO | Wants evidence | Return on any new spend |
| Chief AI Officer | Wants control | Governance, trust, auditability of AI decisions |

## Hypotheses to test in discovery (NOT facts)

1. Forecasts miss at the store/distributor level, causing stockouts on some doors and overproduction or markdowns on others.
2. Promotion decisions rely on last year's results and rep judgment.
3. Planning data lives in several systems and is reconciled by hand each week.
4. Previous "AI" pilots stalled because nobody trusted recommendations they couldn't explain.
5. Nobody currently measures whether a recommendation, once acted on, produced an outcome.

If discovery disproves two or more of these, **do not run the planned demo.** Re-scope it.
