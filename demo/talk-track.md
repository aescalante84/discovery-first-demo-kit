# 45-minute talk track

Built after discovery. Replace `[...]` with what the buyer actually said. If a segment doesn't map to a pain they confirmed, **cut it.**

## Structure

| Time | Segment | Maps to |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Recap their three priorities, in their words | Discovery bullets |
| 0:03 | Moment 1: the weekly plan, before and after | Current-state pain |
| 0:15 | Moment 2: a recommendation, opened up (reasons, math, confidence) | Trust / governance pain |
| 0:25 | Moment 3: approval and write-back of outcomes | "Nobody measures outcomes" pain |
| 0:33 | The proof proposal | Finance and AI governance |
| 0:40 | Questions, then next step with a date | Decision process |

## Each moment follows the same shape

1. **Their words:** "You told us [quote]."
2. **The moment:** one screen, one action, under 3 minutes. `[screen / click path goes here]`
3. **The proof point:** what changed, in numbers they can check.
4. **The check:** "Is that the problem you meant?"

## The recommendation screen (Moment 2)

This is the screen that wins or loses a skeptical room. It must show, for one recommendation:

- **What** is recommended
- **Why**: the drivers, in plain language
- **The math**: the inputs and how they combine
- **Confidence**: and what happens when it's low (it routes to a person)
- **Who approved it**, and when

If the product can't show all five, say so, and say what you'd show instead. Do not improvise.

## Do not demo

- Features nobody asked about
- Any number you can't trace to an input the buyer gave you
- A modeled figure presented as a forecast. Always: "This is sizing, not a promise. Here's how we'd prove it."
- Admin and configuration screens unless a CTO asks

## Closing the meeting

> "Based on what we saw, would a 6-week proof on [scope] be the right next step? I'll send the plan tomorrow. Who else should be on the review?"

Leave with: a date, a named reviewer from Finance, and a named reviewer from AI/governance.
