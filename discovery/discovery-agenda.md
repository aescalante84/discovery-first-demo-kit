# 15-minute discovery agenda (inside a 60-minute meeting)

The goal is not to qualify. It is to leave the room with the buyer's own words for the problem, so every minute of the demo that follows maps to something they said.

## 0:00 Frame (2 min)

> "I have about 40 minutes of demo prepared, but I'd rather show you only what matters. Can I take 15 minutes to ask a few questions first, and then I'll cut the demo down to what's relevant?"

Listening for: whether anyone objects. A seller pushing to skip discovery is a signal to agree the agenda with them *before* the meeting.

## 0:02 Current state (4 min)

- Walk me through how a weekly plan gets made today, from data to decision.
- Where do people override the numbers, and why?
- What did the last miss look like? Who found out, and when?

Listening for: manual reconciliation, spreadsheet handoffs, "tribal knowledge," named people who are single points of failure.

## 0:06 Cost of the problem (4 min)

- When forecasts miss, what does it cost: stockouts, markdowns, expedited freight, lost shelf space?
- Has anyone put a number on it? Whose number is it?
- What happens if nothing changes this year?

Listening for: a number, a name attached to the number, and a deadline.

## 0:10 Decision process (3 min)

- If this worked, who would need to say yes? Who would need to not say no?
- Have you piloted anything like this before? What happened?
- What would make Finance and your AI/governance owner comfortable?

Listening for: the real approvers, past pilot scars, the evidence bar.

## 0:13 Agree what to see (2 min)

> "Here's what I heard: [3 bullets, in their words]. I'll show you those three things and skip the rest. Did I get that right?"

Write the three bullets on screen. The demo is now a response to them, not a tour.

## After the meeting

Send the three bullets back in an email within the hour, plus a proposed proof (see `/proof`). Do this even if the deal is not yet qualified.
